Volunteering and public service jobs

Showing up at meetings is one way to take part. Giving your time, or making public service your work, is another.

Volunteer

  • Serve Idaho is the Governor's commission on service and volunteerism. It runs AmeriCorps programs in Idaho and points people toward volunteer opportunities around the state.
  • Work the polls. County clerks hire poll workers for every election. It is paid, it takes one long day, and clerks are almost always short of people. Contact your county elections office.
  • Your library, school and parks. Most take volunteers directly. Friends of the Library groups and parent organizations are easy places to start.
  • Search and rescue, fire and ambulance. Much of rural Idaho depends on volunteer responders, usually organized through the sheriff's office or the local fire district.
  • State parks and Fish and Game both run volunteer programs, from campground hosts to habitat projects.

Serve on a board

Planning commissions, library boards and state advisory councils are filled by residents. See serve on a board or commission.

Work in government

  • State jobs. The Idaho Division of Human Resources lists openings across state agencies and explains how hiring works.
  • City, county and school district jobs are posted on each government's own website.
  • IdahoWorks, run by the Idaho Department of Labor, lists public and private jobs statewide.
  • Federal jobs in Idaho, including with the Forest Service, the Bureau of Land Management and the Idaho National Laboratory's federal offices, are posted on USAJOBS.
  • The Legislature hires session staff each winter, including committee secretaries and pages. High school seniors can apply to be pages.

Run for something

Many local seats go uncontested. See running for office.
