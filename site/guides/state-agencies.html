Which state agency does what

A short directory for the most common needs, and what to do when you can't get an answer.

Everyday needs

  • Health coverage, food assistance, child support, child protection: Department of Health and Welfare. Dialing 2-1-1 reaches the Idaho CareLine, which connects people to health and human services statewide.
  • Unemployment, job search, unpaid wages: Department of Labor.
  • Driver's licenses and vehicle registration: Idaho Transportation Department sets the rules and offers online services. In person, licenses are handled at county sheriff's offices and registration at county assessor's offices.
  • State income tax, sales tax, property tax relief: State Tax Commission.
  • Scams, bad business practices, open meetings and public records questions: Attorney General.
  • Insurance complaints: Department of Insurance.
  • Professional and contractor licenses, and complaints about a licensed professional: Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.
  • Discrimination at work, in housing or in public places: Idaho Human Rights Commission.
  • Voting, elections, business filings: Secretary of State.

Land, water and the outdoors

  • Hunting and fishing licenses, wildlife: Department of Fish and Game.
  • State parks, boat and off-road vehicle stickers: Department of Parks and Recreation.
  • Water rights and wells: Department of Water Resources.
  • Air and water quality, spills: Department of Environmental Quality.
  • Electric, gas and water utility rates: Public Utilities Commission, which takes public comment on rate cases.

About two-thirds of Idaho is federal land. National forests, Bureau of Land Management ground and national parks are run by federal agencies, not the state. See working with federal agencies.

Schools and public safety

  • K-12 schools: your local school district first, then the State Department of Education.
  • Colleges and universities: the State Board of Education.
  • State prisons, probation and parole: Department of Correction. County jails are run by the sheriff.
  • State highways and troopers: the Transportation Department and the Idaho State Police.

When you're stuck

  1. Ask for a supervisor, and get names and dates in writing.
  2. Use the agency's formal process. Most decisions about benefits, licenses and permits come with a right to appeal and a deadline. The deadline is on the notice.
  3. Call your legislators. They can ask an agency about a constituent's case. See Who represents you.
  4. Contact the Governor's office, which takes constituent requests about state agencies.
  5. Ask for the records. You can request your own file, or any public record, under the Public Records Act.

Rules, not just laws

Agencies write rules that fill in the details of state law. Proposed rules are published in the monthly Idaho Administrative Bulletin with a public comment period, and the Legislature reviews them each session. If a rule is the problem, that comment period is the time to say so.
